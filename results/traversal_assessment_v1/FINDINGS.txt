STRONGER TRAVERSAL-BASELINE ASSESSMENT

Two matched W24 RTL simulations completed: original C07 and conventional early
suppression of unselected PixelShuffle outputs. No FPGA programming, synthesis
or route was performed. All 20 integrated requests passed, including 614,400
selected output-word comparisons and twenty total fault/reuse checks.
The frozen cases use one archived development image, masks [0,2] and [57,59],
both skipping states and identical input/output backpressure.

The original formatter expands unselected positions before discarding pixels.
The candidate keeps positional input/feature scanning unchanged but bypasses
top-pixel emission and bottom-pair read/emission for unselected positions.
It still advances the bottom scanner one column per cycle. This is a partial
conventional baseline challenge, not an ideal free gap-jumping accelerator.

For [0,2] with skipping enabled, raster loop cycles fall from 1,733,354 to
1,065,463 (38.53%); merged cycles fall from 390,942 to 387,980 (0.76%). Without
skipping, raster falls 33.13% and merged 0.41%. The raster-minus-merged cycle
gap falls 49.53% with skipping and 49.50% without it. Box reductions range
1.79--3.11% and merged reductions range 0.21--0.76% on the two tested masks.

The physical region advantage remains valid for the measured engine, but is
not all unavoidable input-gap or neural arithmetic cost. Unnecessary output
formatting contributes substantially in this sparse probe. Residual scanner
cost remains. No physical complete-service gain or routed feasibility follows
from this RTL comparison alone. Selected pixels and arithmetic are unchanged.

Decision: HOLD arithmetic parallelism. Another lane count does not resolve the
demonstrated baseline weakness. If further hardware is pursued, validating this
conventional output-suppression control has priority. The current contribution
is stronger explanation and competitive framing, not a new sparse mechanism.
